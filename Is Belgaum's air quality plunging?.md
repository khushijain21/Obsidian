Lately Delhi, our national capital, has come to be known as the gas chamber or Chernobyl of India because of its terrible state of air quality.

But air pollution is not a unique problem to Delhi. 11 major cities in India has not experienced "good" air  in the last decade. "good" air is when AQI (air quality index) is below 50. In the list of world's 100 most polluted cities, 94 are from India. Somehow, breathing toxic air has become the new normal.

We might believe Belgaum is doing better as a Tier-2 city but the data looks in the opposite direction. The average AQI has increased by 19% every year since 2020. And in 2022, Belgaum was ranked the most polluted city in Karnataka, ahead of Bengaluru.

And December, so far, has experienced an average AQI of 150 which is considered "unhealthy" by WHO standards. This is equivalent to smoking 3.3 cigarettes a day. At this level, "everyone" experiences adverse effects and not just sensitive groups. To put this number into perspective, New York usually has an AQI below 16, so if you ever wondered why skies abroad look so "blue", now you know why.

The major pollutant is PM10 and PM2.5 caused by vehicular emissions, grass burning,  construction dust and  industrial emissions. The long term effects of breathing air at this level can cause inflammation of the lungs, hypertension, cognitive decline etc.

It can feel very to easy to be defeated by these numbers and believe this is a lost cause  but science has shown this trend can be reversed by right policy and public changes. The science to fix air pollution has always been around, what is lacking is the political intent to fix it.
#### What can you  personally do
- Talk to your neighbours who burn dry leaves in winter. Start a community drive to start composting kitchen waste and dry leaves instead of burning it.
- Use public transport, bicycle or e-vehicles for moving around. If public transport does not exist where you live, talk to your local authorities and push for these changes. 
- Construction sites must be adequately covered to prevent dust out. Push for stricter patrolling to ensures construction sites meets compliance.
- Do not burn Crackers
- Talk more about it to your local authorities, political leaders and change-makers. 

This problem requires a multi pronged solution and nobody deserves to breathe toxic air. Let's not raise a sick generation only because we couldn't push for policies that are not popular.
